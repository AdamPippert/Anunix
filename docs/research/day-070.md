# Day 070: Reserve shared exposure before an external effect

The September 3 briefing treats interference and irreversible exposure as distinct costs imposed on other actors.
At comparison baseline `8a0261e`, Anunix tracks protected-operation outcomes but has no shared exposure budget.
This step adds hierarchical accounting to explicitly budgeted protected operations.
Unknown outcomes retain their charge after authority revocation.

## Scope

The source is [Day 070 in the daily review](daily-review.md#day-070--2026-09-03).
The source message identifier is `d1a26ace-fae8-4748-ba29-81c5edc427bd`.
The briefing discusses SchedBlame, MeanField, the Irreversibility Budget, and EffectBound.
It recommends separate dimensions for performance interference and exposure from external effects.
This experiment selects shared exposure accounting and the distinction between revocation and settlement.
It does not reproduce CPU attribution, GPU interference modeling, or economic risk estimates.

## Comparison

Day 055 copies a protected request and checks its source, Sink, provider generation, and execution authority.
The provider's result becomes committed or unknown, and an unknown operation cannot silently retry.
Day 067 retains completion records across worker replacement.
Those mechanisms do not constrain the aggregate exposure of several individually permitted effects.

The [exposure API](../../kernel/include/anx/exposure.h) defines immutable limits in controller-assigned units.
The [protected-operation implementation](../../kernel/core/exec/external.c) maintains the ledgers under its existing registry lock.
One reservation checks every ancestor before changing any balance.
The controller assigns the charge to the exact copied operation through the budgeted preparation API.

## Implementation

The registry permits 32 ledgers with at most eight levels per hierarchy.
Each ledger retains its owner and an immutable parent relationship.
A child owner must belong to the parent's Cell ancestry.
The child limit cannot exceed its parent's limit.
Each operation must use a leaf ledger belonging to its exact owner.

The root can represent a principal, with workflow and agent ledgers beneath it.
The controller defines the meaning of one exposure unit for that hierarchy.
The amount is not a monetary estimate or a conversion from CPU time.
Active Cells cannot create ledgers, change limits, assign charges, revoke ledgers, or discard protected operations.

| Operation boundary | Ledger change across every ancestor |
| --- | --- |
| Successful preparation | Reserve the immutable charge |
| Pre-dispatch cancellation | Return the reserved charge |
| Provider entry | Count one issued dispatch; retain its reservation |
| Confirmed provider result | Move the charge from reserved to committed |
| Ambiguous provider result | Move the charge from reserved to uncertain |
| Committed-record cleanup | Retain the committed charge |

Admission rejects a charge that exceeds any ancestor's remaining capacity.
It preserves every balance and the caller's output identifier on rejection.
The amount must be positive, and subtraction from remaining capacity avoids arithmetic overflow.
The private operation record retains the ledger and charge; a copied public view grants no authority.

Dispatch checks ancestor revocation before the existing effect fence admits the provider call.
The registry marks the issued dispatch before calling the real provider.
Reentry and cleanup remain blocked during that call.
Settlement updates the original hierarchy even after revocation.
An uncertain operation retains its ledger reference and cannot refund its charge through cleanup.

Revocation blocks new reservations and previously prepared dispatches.
It does not erase an issued operation or reduce its exposure.
The view's `closed` flag requires effective revocation and no reserved, issued, or uncertain operations in that ledger's subtree.
Confirmed charges remain consumed after that local closure.
Destroying an eligible child ledger does not refund its ancestors' confirmed charges.

Only the exact owner or controller can inspect a ledger.
The controller can destroy a revoked ledger after its children and operation references disappear.
Uncertain charges prevent destruction.
The kernel provides no automatic timeout refund or unverified compensation path.

## Native checks

The [regression](../../kernel/core/tools/research_day070.c) creates a principal, workflow, and three derived agents.
The principal limit is 100 units, the workflow limit is 80 units, and each agent limit is 60 units.
Two 40-unit effects reserve the workflow's full allowance.
The third effect fails before provider execution despite satisfying its individual agent limit.
An oversized charge and an attempt to use another agent's ledger also fail.

Canceling a prepared effect returns 40 units at every level.
Another agent can then reserve those units.
The first admitted provider completes, and its 40-unit charge becomes committed.
Discarding that operation does not restore the charge.
The next provider returns an injected timeout, which retains 40 uncertain units.

| Final shared balance | Principal ledger | Workflow ledger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Limit | 100 units | 80 units |
| Reserved | 0 units | 0 units |
| Committed | 40 units | 40 units |
| Uncertain | 40 units | 40 units |
| Closed after revocation | No | No |

The fixture removes eligible child ledgers and checks that the parent balances remain unchanged.
The uncertain operation, its remaining ancestry, and its source remain retained for the rest of the boot.
That retained state prevents a false refund or closure claim.

Two separate cases exercise revocation around dispatch.
Revocation before admission prevents the provider call and retains the reservation until controller cancellation.
A research-only hook revokes another ledger after dispatch admission and before its synchronous provider callback.
The callback observes a revoked ledger with an outstanding dispatch and `closed=false`.
Its confirmed completion then permits local closure while preserving the committed charge.

Foreign active Cells cannot inspect a ledger or dispatch its operation.
Active owners cannot assign their own prices or revoke their budgets.
The real provider also checks the recorded charge and rejects duplicate dispatch through the existing operation state machine.

## Limits

The controller assigns charges; the kernel checks arithmetic and composition, not the quality of risk estimates.
Underpriced effects, missing effect classes, and correlated losses remain outside this experiment.
Different exposure measures need separate hierarchies rather than an invented universal currency.

The budgeted preparation API enrolls an operation explicitly.
Existing unbudgeted protected operations and legacy external calls remain outside that accounting contract.
This step does not claim universal enforcement across every provider path or continuation.
It adds no CPU interference tracer, learned scheduler, pricing model, or automatic approval mechanism.

The `closed` flag describes the local protected-operation registry.
A successful callback does not certify that a remote provider has no queued or downstream work.
Provider-wide effect closure still requires a provider-specific protocol and evidence beyond this ledger.
The native providers execute synchronously and create no downstream carriers.

The ledgers and operation records remain within the current boot.
They provide no persistence, replication, reconciliation, or crash recovery.
The retained unknown test case consumes three ledger slots per invocation and deliberately resists ordinary cleanup.
Production builds omit the revocation fault hook.
